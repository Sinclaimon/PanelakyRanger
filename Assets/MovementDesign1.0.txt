SKIING MOVEMENT SYSTEM (IDEA NOTES)

Goal
- Downhill skiing movement focused on flow, rhythm, and line choice.
- Arcade carving with mild powder resistance, satisfying to do repeatedly.
- Speed is earned and maintained, not constant.

Core Feel
- Turning costs a bit of speed (resistance).
- Clean rhythm and good lines convert that cost into acceleration (release).
- The player should feel when they are “in it” without needing UI.

Flow State (implicit tier system)
- Player has a flow tier that influences speed and feedback intensity.
- Flow tier increases after multiple clean sequences, not instantly.
- Missing rhythm or messing up line choice drops tier and reduces speed.
- Variables should be exposed so tier count, gains, and losses can be tuned.

Rhythm
- Flow is built through a satisfying left-right carving rhythm, not button combos.
- Rhythm becomes more demanding as speed increases.
- Success should be primarily felt via response and feedback, UI optional for debugging.

Environment and Line Choice
- Terrain and tree placement create natural lines and rhythm opportunities.
- Threading between trees is a high-value action that strongly contributes to flow tier gain.
- For early prototype, missing a line is a missed opportunity more than a hard fail.
- Later, trees can become higher risk (collision = bigger flow loss).

Feedback Priorities
- Snow spray is essential, and scales with flow tier and carving quality.
- Tracks in snow are essential, and help show line quality and rhythm.
- Camera responds to flow tier (subtle tilt, FOV/speed feeling, smoothness changes).
- Basic audio supports carving quality (scrape/crunch/hiss), but does not explain systems.
- Minimal UI, only to indicate tier changes if needed, keep it subtle.

Player Actions (movement phase)
- Carve left/right, with a strong sense of edge and resistance.
- Dash on the snowboard for repositioning or burst of speed (tunable).
- Movement should encourage maintaining rhythm through terrain rather than spamming dash.

Success Definition (movement-only)
- A player can enter flow, feel the difference, and try to maintain it.
- Threading trees and carving rhythm are satisfying on their own.
- The system supports experimentation with how harsh or forgiving turns and mistakes are.

Tuning Questions (for iteration)
- How much speed does a hard turn cost?
- How many clean sequences to regain a tier?
- How punishing is a missed rhythm or bad line?
- How strong is the tree-threading reward?
- How much do camera and VFX amplify the sensation of flow?

Non-goals for now
- No tricks, no complex animations, no fishing systems.
- Build the fun movement loop first, then layer in everything else.
